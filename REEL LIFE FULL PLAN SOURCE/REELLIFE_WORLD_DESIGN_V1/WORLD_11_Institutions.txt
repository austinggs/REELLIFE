WORLD 11 — INSTITUTIONS

Defines persistent social structures performing functions through people, rules, resources, procedures, records, and history.

Core:
- Institution ≠ current employees.
- Organization vs institution distinction.
- Institutional identity, types, functions, authority, procedures, rules, workload, performance.
- Institutions can act through authorized representatives.
- Legal authority ≠ operational capacity.
- Memory through records/databases/archives/procedures/precedents/traditions/training/buildings/artifacts.
- Memory can be lost through war, disaster, corruption, leadership, budget, technology failure.
- Records can be accurate, incomplete, outdated, corrupted, missing, disputed, forged.
- Precedent, culture, reputation, trust, legitimacy.
- Creation/change/merger/split/failure/succession.
- Public/private/hybrid institutions; hierarchy, dependency, competition, cooperation, conflict.
- Educational, healthcare, financial, legal, media, research, cultural, religious, professional institutions.
- Institutional networks, infrastructure dependencies, corruption, reform, resilience, accessibility, geography, knowledge, secrecy, transparency.
- History/legacy, data model, resolution, lived player interactions, validation.

Core principle: Institutions let society remember, coordinate, and act beyond individual lifespans; people operate institutions, but institutions can outlive operators.
