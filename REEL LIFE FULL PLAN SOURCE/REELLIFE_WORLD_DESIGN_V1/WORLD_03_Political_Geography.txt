WORLD 03 — POLITICAL GEOGRAPHY

Defines political territories, borders, jurisdictions, administrative divisions, sovereignty, and territorial control.

Core:
- Political geography layered onto physical geography.
- Hierarchy: Planet → Continent → Country/Territory → Administrative Region → District → Municipality/City → Neighborhood.
- Countries, dependent/disputed/admin territories, borders, crossings, jurisdictions, capitals, territorial claims, historical borders.
- Sovereignty categories include sovereign, autonomous, dependent, occupied, disputed, administered, protectorate, special administrative, unrecognized, neutral, international.
- Territorial claim ≠ effective control ≠ recognized sovereignty.
- Effective control ≠ formal jurisdiction.
- Borders affect legal, tax, immigration, customs, transport, communication, policing, healthcare, education, employment, trade, identity, information.
- Internal/overlapping jurisdictions, capitals, enclaves/exclaves, special zones.
- Historical political geography can change through war, treaties, administration, and political change.
- Data-driven, discoverable, validated, dynamically changeable.

Core principle: Political geography defines who claims, controls, administers, and governs space; these do not necessarily belong to the same actor.
