WORLD 06 — NEIGHBORHOODS

Defines neighborhoods as immediate physical, social, economic, cultural, and historical contexts.

Core:
- Physical area + population + built/social/economic environment + history.
- Hierarchy continues to property/building/unit/room.
- NeighborhoodId, official/common/historical names, boundaries, parent settlement/district, development period, land use, population, housing, institutions, infrastructure, economy, culture, reputation, history.
- Administrative, social, physical, economic, perceptual boundaries.
- Housing stock, affordability, population composition/turnover, social networks, community organizations.
- Local institutions, schools, employment, commerce, specialization.
- Culture/language/religion as patterns, not determinants.
- Reputation distinct from objective conditions; safety distinct from reporting.
- Infrastructure, accessibility, transport, public space, local events.
- Development/redevelopment/displacement/gentrification can emerge.
- History, generational continuity, inequality, interdependence, resolution, validation.

Core principle: The neighborhood is where large-scale world conditions become immediate lived context.
