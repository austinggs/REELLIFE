WORLD 07 — WORLD HISTORY

Defines history as accumulated causality explaining why the world currently looks as it does.

Core:
- Deep, early human, civilizational, modern, and recent history.
- Canonical history = what happened; historical belief/narrative is distinct.
- Reality → information → interpretation → memory → belief.
- Events use Event Engine; gradual changes use processes.
- Multiple causes/consequences and turning points.
- Lost/uncertain history; archives, records, monuments, oral traditions.
- Historical figures are ordinary Persons with historical importance.
- Organizations can form, grow, split, merge, disappear, and leave successors.
- Histories of cities, borders, migration, diasporas, culture, religion, economy, technology, science, politics, social movements.
- War is a process with aftermath/reconstruction.
- Historical memory, revision, secrets, artifacts, property, institutional and economic inheritance, generational consequences.
- Historical inequality, alternate histories, authored/emergent/sandbox/scenario modes.
- Historical compression by relevance/resolution; discovery through education, media, museums, conversations, archives, artifacts.

Core principle: The world remembers what happened even if people do not; history is accumulated causality.
