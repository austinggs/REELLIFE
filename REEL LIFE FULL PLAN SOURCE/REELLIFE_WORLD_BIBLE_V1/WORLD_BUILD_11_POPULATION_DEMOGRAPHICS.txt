WORLD BUILD 11 — POPULATION & DEMOGRAPHICS
AURELIA WORLD BIBLE V1
============================================================

Global population
-----------------
Aurelia begins with approximately 6.8 billion people.

Continental population targets
------------------------------
Elandra: ~1.35 billion
Veyra: ~1.55 billion
Sahraen: ~1.25 billion
Orinth: ~1.10 billion

Ilyra: ~0.70 billion
Total: ~6.80 billion

Population distribution
-----------------------
Population is concentrated in:
- river basins
- fertile agricultural regions
- temperate coasts
- industrial corridors
- major ports
- established metropolitan regions

Low-density environments include:
- deserts
- high mountains
- tundra
- dense rainforest interiors
- remote islands
- extreme northern regions

----------------------
- age structure
- fertility
- mortality
- life expectancy
- household size and composition
- births and deaths
- marriage and cohabitation patterns
- urbanization

Age structure
-------------
Population is tracked through age cohorts including infants, children, adolescents, young adults, adults, middle-aged adults and elderly populations.

Births and fertility
--------------------
Births emerge from population age structure, fertility rates, relationships, household formation, healthcare access, economic conditions, cultural context and individual reproductive decisions.

Mortality
---------
Deaths emerge from age, health, disease, accidents, disaster exposure, violence, environmental conditions, healthcare access and conflict.

Life expectancy
---------------
Average life expectancy differs by country and region because of healthcare, nutrition, sanitation, income, education, environment and safety. Individual lifespan remains variable.

Households
----------
Household types include:
- single-person
- couple
- family with children
- multigenerational
- extended-family
- shared housing
- student housing
- communal living
- institutional households

Household formation can result from marriage, partnership, leaving home, migration, education, employment, divorce, separation, widowhood, family decisions and housing availability.

Urbanization
------------
Approximately 70% of Aurelia's population is urban or metropolitan at game start, with substantial country-level variation.

Rural populations remain economically important through farming, livestock, forestry, fishing, resource extraction and remote community life.

Migration
---------
Internal migration is driven by jobs, education, housing, family, marriage, disasters, conflict, climate and opportunity.

International migration is shaped by employment, education, family reunification, safety, political conditions, economics and legal eligibility.

Forced displacement can result from war, persecution, disaster, famine, instability and environmental collapse.

Diaspora
--------
Migration creates long-lived diaspora networks that can preserve language, religion, food, businesses, family connections and cultural practices while also evolving over generations.

Education and labor demographics
-------------------------------
Age structure affects demand for schools, teachers, universities and vocational training. Labor-force size depends on working-age population, health, education, participation, migration and retirement.

Dependency
----------
The simulation tracks broad dependency relationships between children, working-age adults and elderly populations.

Population interactions
-----------------------
Population interacts with housing, infrastructure, economy, culture, religion, technology, environment and institutions.

Key feedback loop:
Population -> Households -> Labor / Education / Consumption -> Economy -> Housing / Infrastructure -> Living Conditions -> Fertility / Mortality / Migration -> Population

Demographic equation
--------------------
Population Change = Births - Deaths + Immigration - Emigration

At regional levels, internal migration is also represented.

Demographic transition
----------------------
Countries may move through different combinations of:
- high births and high deaths
- falling mortality
- rapid population growth
- declining fertility
- stabilization
- aging

Countries need not follow identical trajectories.

Generational cohorts
--------------------
Cohorts can be shaped by wars, economic booms, disasters, technological shifts, pandemics and migration waves. Those experiences can persist across decades.

Aggregate simulation
--------------------
At low resolution, populations are represented statistically. When relevance rises, households and individuals can be materialized while preserving demographic context and causal history.
