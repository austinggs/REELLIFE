WORLD BUILD 01 — PLANET & GLOBAL GEOGRAPHY
AURELIA WORLD BIBLE V1
============================================================

1. Planet Identity
-------------------
Planet name: Aurelia
Scale: Earth-scale terrestrial world
Coordinate model: latitude, longitude, elevation, depth

Aurelia is a fully fictional world. It is not dependent on a real-world map and must remain internally coherent, spatially persistent, and causally meaningful.

2. Global Geography
--------------------
Aurelia contains six major continents:
- Elandra
- Veyra
- Sahraen
- Orinth
- Kharos
- Ilyra

The planet contains five major oceans:
- Meridian Ocean
- Western Ocean
- Eastern Ocean
- Southern Ocean
- Polar Ocean

Additional seas, bays, gulfs, inland waters and island groups can exist within the detailed geographic layer.

3. Major Mountain Systems
--------------------------
- Crown Mountains
- Veyran Spine
- Kharic Range
- Southern Wall

Mountain systems shape climate, drainage, transportation, settlement, borders, resources, hazards and historical development.

4. Major River Systems
----------------------
- Ardan River
- Veyr River
- Saren River
- Orin River
- Khar River

River systems influence agriculture, transport, settlement, trade, political boundaries and historic state formation.

5. Climate and Ecology
----------------------
Aurelia contains tropical, subtropical, temperate, arid, semi-arid, alpine, subpolar and polar environments. Climate varies with latitude, elevation, ocean currents, terrain and rain-shadow effects.

Ecological regions include forests, grasslands, savannahs, deserts, wetlands, rainforests, alpine zones, tundra, coastal ecosystems and island ecosystems.

6. Major Resource Geography
---------------------------
Resource categories include:
- energy resources: petroleum, natural gas, coal, uranium, hydropower potential, solar potential, wind potential, geothermal potential
- metals and minerals: iron, copper, aluminum-bearing ores, nickel, tin, zinc, precious metals, rare industrial minerals
- construction materials: limestone, clay, sand, gravel, stone, timber
- biological resources: forests, fisheries, livestock, crops, medicinal plants
- water resources: rivers, lakes, groundwater, reservoirs and desalination potential

Resources are spatially authoritative. Their availability is not assumed to equal affordability, production capacity or market access.

7. Hazards
----------
Major hazards include floods, droughts, storms, wildfires, earthquakes, volcanic activity, landslides, extreme heat, environmental degradation and pollution.

Hazard impact follows the broader model:
Hazard -> Exposure -> Vulnerability -> Impact -> Response -> Recovery

8. Ocean and Atmospheric Systems
--------------------------------
Ocean currents, prevailing winds, elevation, coastlines and rain-shadow effects influence regional climates and agriculture. Coastal and maritime regions are especially shaped by ocean access, fisheries, storms and shipping routes.

9. Islands, Peninsulas and Chokepoints
--------------------------------------
Aurelia contains islands, peninsulas, inland seas and strategic geographic bottlenecks. These locations affect maritime trade, migration, defense, culture and regional integration.

10. Geographic Corridors
------------------------
Major geographic and economic corridors include:
- Ardan Corridor
- Veyran Corridor
- Saren Corridor
- Orin Corridor
- Kharic Corridor
- Ilyran Maritime Corridor

11. Geographic Authority
------------------------
Geography is authoritative simulation state. Maps are presentation layers over that state.

The map must not become the source of truth for terrain, borders, infrastructure or settlements.

13. Validation Rules
--------------------
World geography should preserve:
- plausible hydrology
- coherent climate zones
- plausible settlement locations
- plausible resource distribution
- navigable and geographically coherent transport routes
- meaningful hazard exposure
- internally consistent boundaries and terrain

14. Design Principle
--------------------
Geography is causal, not decorative. It should explain or constrain settlement, agriculture, transport, economics, culture, political history and environmental risk.
